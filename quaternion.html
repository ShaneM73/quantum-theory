Quaternion sphere

The unit sphere of pure quaternions xi + yj + zk, multiplied by q, then stereographically projected from −1 into 3D, as in the 3Blue1Brown video.

Unit quaternions with real part w form a sphere of radius √(1−w²). Projected (before multiplying), its radius is √((1−w)/(1+w)).

1The projection

A unit quaternion w + xi + yj + zk lands at (x, y, z)/(1 + w). So 1 sits at the origin, −1 is at infinity, and points with w = 0 lie exactly on the unit sphere.

2Becoming a plane

In q · p mode, at θ = 90° we have q = i. The point i on the sphere becomes i·i = −1, flies off to infinity, and the sphere is stretched into a flat plane.

3180° versus 360°

At θ = 180°, q = −1. The sphere has its shape back, but every point has moved to the opposite side and the pink point 1 is at infinity. Only at 360° is everything truly home.

43D rotations

Switch to q p q⁻¹. Multiplying on the left turns two planes at once; multiplying by q⁻¹ on the right turns the 1–u plane back and turns the other plane once more. The result is a rotation by 2θ about u, which is where the half angle comes from.
