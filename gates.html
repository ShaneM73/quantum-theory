Gates and quaternions

Every single-qubit gate (up to a global phase) is a unit quaternion q = cos(α/2) + n sin(α/2). It rotates the Bloch sphere by α about n.

1Gates are rotations

X turns |0⟩ into |1⟩, like a classical NOT. H turns |0⟩ to |+⟩ on the equator, half 0 and half 1. S and T only rotate about z, so they change the relative phase (the longitude).

2Why α/2

A 3D vector is rotated by q p q⁻¹: once on the left, once with q⁻¹ on the right, so the plane perpendicular to the axis turns twice. To turn the sphere by α, q can only contain α/2.

3A minus sign at 360°

Drag α to 360°. The arrow is back where it started, but q = −1 and both a and b have flipped sign (see the two dials above). Only at 720° is q back to 1. One turn of q = two turns of the sphere. Electron spin behaves exactly like this.

4Can the sign be seen?

For a single qubit on its own, an overall −1 cannot be measured. But if only half of a superposition is turned by 360°, that half's minus sign interferes with the other half and becomes measurable. Neutron interferometry experiments have observed it.
